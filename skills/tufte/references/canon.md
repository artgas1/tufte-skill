# Tufte canon — verified quotes and page numbers

Use this file when you cite Tufte. Every entry below was checked against the text of the book
(scans or text layers of the editions named here) during this skill's development. If a principle
you want to cite is not here, state it **without** a page number rather than guessing one.

Abbreviations and editions:
- **VDQI** — *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, 2nd ed. (2001).
- **EI** — *Envisioning Information* (1990).
- **VE** — *Visual Explanations* (1997).
- **BE** — *Beautiful Evidence* (2006).
- **CSPP** — *The Cognitive Style of PowerPoint*, 2nd ed. (2006); page numbers of the essay, not of BE.

## Graphical integrity (VDQI ch. 2)

- **Lie Factor** = size of effect shown in graphic / size of effect in data. "Lie Factors greater
  than 1.05 or less than .95 indicate substantial distortion" (p. 57). "Lie Factors of two to five
  are not uncommon" (p. 57).
- Worked example (pp. 57–58): fuel-economy standards rise 18 → 27.5 mpg (+53%); the drawn lines grow
  0.6 → 5.3 in (+783%); LF = 783 / 53 = **14.8**.
- Other measured cases: 9.4 and 9.5 (oil prices, p. 62), 2.8 (p. 69), 59.4 (p. 71).
- Perceived area grows roughly as (actual area)^x with x ≈ .8 ± .3 (p. 55) — another reason not to
  encode magnitude by area.
- The six principles (p. 77, each also stated earlier in the chapter):
  1. "The representation of numbers, as physically measured on the surface of the graphic itself,
     should be directly proportional to the numerical quantities represented." (also p. 56)
  2. "Clear, detailed, and thorough labeling should be used to defeat graphical distortion and
     ambiguity. Write out explanations of the data on the graphic itself. Label important events in
     the data." (also p. 56)
  3. "Show data variation, not design variation." (also p. 61)
  4. "In time-series displays of money, deflated and standardized units of monetary measurement are
     nearly always better than nominal units." (also p. 68)
  5. "The number of information-carrying (variable) dimensions depicted should not exceed the
     number of dimensions in the data." (also p. 71)
  6. "Graphics must not quote data out of context." (also p. 74, with the question "compared to
     what?")

## Graphical excellence (VDQI ch. 1)

- "Graphical displays should" — show the data; induce thought about substance rather than
  methodology or design; avoid distorting what the data have to say; present many numbers in a
  small space; … (p. 13).
- "Graphical excellence is that which gives to the viewer the greatest number of ideas in the
  shortest time with the least ink in the smallest space." It "is nearly always multivariate. And
  graphical excellence requires telling the truth about the data." (p. 51)

## Data-ink and chartjunk (VDQI ch. 4–6)

- "Above all else show the data." (p. 92; restated p. 105)
- Data-ink is "the non-erasable core of a graphic, the non-redundant ink arranged in response to
  variation in the numbers represented"; data-ink ratio = data-ink / total ink (p. 93).
- The five principles (p. 105): Above all else show the data. Maximize the data-ink ratio. Erase
  non-data-ink. Erase redundant data-ink. Revise and edit.
- Chartjunk chapter title: "Vibrations, Grids, and Ducks" (p. 107). Moiré vibration from hatching
  and patterns (pp. 107–112).
- "Dark grid lines are chartjunk. They carry no information, clutter up the graphic, and generate
  graphic activity unrelated to data information." (p. 113) Grids "should be muted relative to the
  data. A gray grid works well" (p. 116).
- The "duck": a graphic "taken over by decorative forms or computer debris" (p. 116). The warning
  "decorate construction, never construct decoration" is **Pugin's**, quoted by Venturi et al.
  (p. 117).
- "Chartjunk can turn bores into disasters, but it can never rescue a thin data set." (p. 121)
- Range-frame: the frame trimmed to the range of the data, showing min and max (pp. 130–132);
  quartile plot (p. 132); dot-dash-plot (p. 133).
- Multifunctioning elements: "Mobilize every graphical element, perhaps several times over, to show
  the data." (p. 139)
- The two-column "table-graphic" ranking countries in 1970 and 1979 with connecting lines
  (pp. 158–159) is the design later called a **slopegraph**; the name is not used in VDQI.

## Density, multiples, tables, proportion (VDQI ch. 8–9)

- "The Shrink Principle": "Graphics can be shrunk way down." (p. 169) Repeated, it leads to small
  multiples (p. 169).
- "Small multiples resemble the frames of a movie: a series of graphics, showing the same
  combination of variables, indexed by changes in another variable." (p. 170) They are "inevitably
  comparative", "deftly multivariate", "drawn almost entirely with data-ink" (p. 175).
- "Tables are preferable to graphics for many small data sets. A table is nearly always better than
  a dumb pie chart; the only worse design than a pie chart is several of them … pie charts should
  never be used." (p. 178)
- "Graphics should tend toward the horizontal, greater in length than height" (p. 186). "If the
  nature of the data suggests the shape of the graphic, follow that suggestion. Otherwise, move
  toward horizontal graphics about 50 percent wider than tall" (p. 190). Of Playfair's 89 graphics,
  92 percent are wider than tall (p. 188).

## Envisioning Information

- "Escaping this flatland is the essential task of envisioning information" (ch. 1).
- Micro/macro readings: "to clarify, add detail" (p. 37).
- "Clutter and confusion are failures of design, not attributes of information." (p. 51); the
  chapter "Layering and Separation" opens on p. 53 with the same sentence inverted ("Confusion and
  clutter …").
- Imhof's "first rule of color composition": pure, bright or very strong colours have "loud,
  unbearable effects when they stand unrelieved over large areas" (p. 58).
- 1 + 1 = 3 or more (Josef Albers): two marks create incidental by-products of their partnership
  (p. 61).
- "Information consists of differences that make a difference" (p. 65; the phrase is Gregory
  Bateson's).
- "At the heart of quantitative reasoning is a single question: Compared to what?" (ch. 4, "Small
  Multiples").
- Colour: "Above all, do no harm." (ch. 5, "Color and Information").

## Visual Explanations

- John Snow's cholera map marks the area's **11** community water pumps (VE p. 30; VDQI p. 24:
  "eleven water pumps").

## Beautiful Evidence — the six principles of analytical design

From the chapter on Minard's map (BE pp. 122–139):
1. Comparisons — "Show comparisons, contrasts, differences."
2. Causality, mechanism, structure, explanation.
3. Multivariate analysis — "show more than 1 or 2 variables."
4. Integration of evidence — "Completely integrate words, numbers, images, diagrams."
5. Documentation — "The credibility of an evidence presentation depends significantly on the
   quality and integrity of the authors and their data sources." (p. 132)
6. Content counts most of all (p. 134).

Sparklines are "datawords: data-intense, design-simple, word-sized graphics" (BE, chapter
"Sparklines"). The phrase "approximately right rather than exactly wrong" that appears there is
Tukey's, quoted by Tufte.

## The Cognitive Style of PowerPoint (CSPP)

- Low-resolution slides force "relentless sequentiality"; for serious presentations, replace
  slides with paper handouts "showing words, numbers, data graphics, images together".
- The Columbia Accident Investigation Board: "The Board views the endemic use of PowerPoint
  briefing slides instead of technical papers as an illustration of the problematic methods of
  technical communication at NASA." (CAIB Report vol. 1, 2003, quoted in CSPP).
- Sentences with subjects and verbs beat bullet lists for analysis (the Gerstner/IBM example).

## Not Tufte (attribute correctly)

- Banking to 45°: W. S. Cleveland (Cleveland, McGill & McGill, "The Shape Parameter of a Two-Variable
  Graph", JASA 83, 1988).
- "Approximately right rather than exactly wrong": John Tukey.
- "Differences that make a difference": Gregory Bateson.
- "Decorate construction, never construct decoration": A. W. N. Pugin.

## Known slips to avoid when citing

- Vauthier's colour method is Tufte's **negative** example: "ingenious in idea but not very
  satisfactory in practice" (VDQI p. 154).
- The largest data matrix in Tufte's journal-density table belongs to *Nature* (3780), not NEJM
  (VDQI p. 167).
- Begley & Ellis (Nature 483:531, 2012) confirmed 6 of 53 landmark studies (11%). *Seeing with Fresh
  Eyes* (pp. 111–112) prints "63"; cite the primary source.
